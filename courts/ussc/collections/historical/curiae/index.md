# The Curiae Project

Beginning around 2002, [The Curiae Project](https://web.archive.org/web/20020924130803/http://curiae.law.yale.edu/) at **Yale Law School** began digitizing the records and briefs in important U.S. Supreme Court cases, and it prioritized those efforts by focusing on cases that received coverage by the most texts on constitutional law.

Unfortunately, those digitization efforts stalled within just a few years. The project went offline around 2008, due to being "under construction", and then a few years later, it was described as "undergoing maintenance", and then it simply disappeared altogether.

In the project's own words:

> To determine the order in which we would convert materials, we referenced the following works on constitutional law, noting those cases given primary treatment by the authors (as evidenced by a separate index or case list).

> To score cases, we looked at the percentage of those works — published after a case was decided — that cite that case. Less than two hundred cases were given primary treatment by a majority of these works. … An "n/a" means that the case had not been decided when that particular ranking text was published (and that text was therefore not counted for that case).

In this collection, cases have been tagged with **Curiae** tags, one for each of the fifteen selected works that provided significant coverage.  The more tags, the more **Popular** the case, with **1st** place going to any case discussed in all fifteen works.
