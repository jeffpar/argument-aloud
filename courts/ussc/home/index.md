## Welcome to Argument Aloud

Every U.S. Supreme Court case usually ends with an opinion, but understanding that opinion requires understanding how the case started and what arguments were made.  This website helps tie those threads together.

Arguments come in many forms. There is the initial petition, and if the Court accepts it, that's followed by a series of briefs, which usually culminates in oral argument.  Oral arguments have been routinely recorded since October 1955 and routinely transcribed since October 1968.  Finally, some weeks or months later, an opinion is issued, unless the Court re-scheduled the case for re-argument, dismissed the case as improperly granted or moot, or simply could not reach a decision (if an even number of Justices were deadlocked).

Digital copies of petitions, briefs, transcripts, audio recordings, and opinions tend to be fully available for modern cases, but as you move back through the 20th century, fewer online resources are available.

Feel free to dive in.  Not sure where to start?  Try a random case, such as [Watson v. RNC](/courts/ussc/?term=2025-10&case=24-1260&turn=369).
