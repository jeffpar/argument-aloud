## Why This Website Exists

Every U.S. Supreme Court case usually ends with an opinion, but fully understanding that opinion means understanding how the case started and what arguments were made.  This website helps bring all those threads together.

Arguments come in many forms. There is the initial petition, and if the Court accepts it, that's followed by a series of briefs, which usually culminates in oral argument.  Oral arguments have been routinely recorded since October 1955 and routinely transcribed since October 1968.

Finally, some weeks or months later, an opinion is issued, unless the Court reschedules the case for re-argument, dismisses the case as improperly granted or moot, or is otherwise unable to reach a decision (eg, if an even number of Justices are deadlocked).

Digital copies of all those pieces (petitions, briefs, transcripts, audio recordings, and opinions) tend to be available for modern cases, but they are scattered across different pages and websites.  And as you move back through the 20th century, fewer online resources are available.

Then there are other pieces of information that are critical to have, such as copies of statutes, records from the lower courts, and so on.  These are usually buried in one or more of the briefs (eg, joint appendices), but having links to the relevant pages in the transcripts would be even better.

You can find examples of how such links would work in a few cases here already, such as [Watson v. RNC](/courts/ussc/?term=2025-10&case=24-1260&turn=369).  Dive in and listen!
